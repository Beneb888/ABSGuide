Landmarks

Brisbane's landmarks perfectly reflect its laid-back, subtropical lifestyle. The city wraps around a winding river, which means most of the best sightseeing spots offer incredible waterfront views, lush green parks, or elevated lookouts. From the Mount Coot-tha lookout to a walk along South Bank, Brisbane has it all.

Mount Coot-tha Lookout

Mount Coot-tha is Brisbane's ultimate vantage point. Standing 287 metres above sea level, it delivers a sweeping, postcard-perfect panorama of the entire city. On a clear day, the view stretches across the grid of CBD skyscrapers, follows the winding ribbon of the Brisbane River, and reaches all the way to Moreton Bay and the distant islands. The lookout's atmosphere shifts dramatically depending on when you visit. On Sunrise you have a quite, crisp and peaceful atmosphere. You'll share the deck with early-morning cyclists who have tackled the winding climb up the mountain. Around Midday, the atmosphere shifts to a vibrant, bustling atmosphere with tour buses, families and tourists taking photos. Sunset to Nightfall. This is when the lookout truly shines. As the sun dips behind the mountain, the city lights flicker to life, turning the view into a glowing, romantic backdrop.

Mount Coot-tha Botanic Gardens

Located right at the base of the mountain, the Brisbane Botanic Gardens Mt Coot-tha is a massive, 56-hectare subtropical oasis. It is entirely separate from the City Botanic Gardens downtown and is widely considered Brisbane's premier horticultural display.
The experience feels like walking through different corners of the world, with highlights including:

  • The Tropical Display Dome: A futuristic, lattice-framed geodesic dome that houses a lush jungle of exotic plants, water lilies, and a cascading stream. It maintains a warm, humid ecosystem inside.
  • The Japanese Garden: A serene, meticulously landscaped sanctuary featuring stone paths, quiet ponds, bamboo groves, and bridges. It is the perfect spot for quiet reflection.
  • Bonsai House & Fern House: Specialized pavilions showcasing delicate, decades-old miniature trees and prehistoric-looking ferns.

Mount Coot-tha Planetarium

Sharing the exact same entrance and parking lot as the Botanic Gardens is the Sir Thomas Brisbane Planetarium. It is the largest planetarium in Queensland and provides a brilliant, educational shift from the natural world to the cosmos.
Inside, visitors will find:

  • The Cosmic Skydome The centerpiece of the planetarium—a massive, 12.5-metre hemispherical dome projection theatre that runs immersive astronomical shows and live star tours of the Southern Hemisphere's night sky.
  • Display Zone & Observatory The entry gallery is a free museum filled with interactive space exhibits, scale models of spacecraft, a real fragment of a meteorite, and a life-sized astronaut suit.

Pro Tip: As the Planetarium, Botanic Gardens and Lookout are all on Mount Coot-tha, why not do a day on the mountain?

Southbank

South Bank is Brisbane's vibrant cultural heart and premier riverside playground. Spanning 17 hectares of lush parklands directly opposite the CBD, this beautifully transformed World Expo 88 site is the ultimate expression of Brisbane's outdoor-centric lifestyle. It seamlessly blends world-class dining, rich cultural institutions, and stunning green spaces along the water, making it an absolute must-visit for any itinerary.

  • Streets Beach Australia's only inner-city, man-made swimming beach. It features a sparkling lagoon, real white sand, and tropical plants, all set against a stunning view of the city skyline. It is entirely free to swim and patrolled by lifeguards year-round.
  • The Grand Arbour: A spectacular 1-kilometre pedestrian walkway framed by 443 curling steel tendrils completely covered in vibrant magenta bougainvillea flowers, blooming beautifully year-round.
  • The Wheel of Brisbane: A 60-metre-tall giant Ferris wheel that offers a 10-to-15-minute ride with 360-degree panoramic views of the river and city skyline.

Story Bridge

As Brisbane's most iconic structural landmark, the heritage-listed Story Bridge is a magnificent fixture of the city skyline. Opened in 1940 after being built as a major public works project during the Great Depression, it holds the title of Australia's longest steel cantilever bridge. Connecting the nightlife hub of Fortitude Valley to the dramatic cliffs of Kangaroo Point, the bridge carries over 100,000 vehicles daily alongside a dedicated, scenic pedestrian pathway. For the adventurous traveller, it offers the thrill of the Story Bridge Adventure Climb, which is one of only three guided bridge climbs in the entire world. Every evening, the massive steel structure is illuminated by a dynamic display of coloured LED lights, creating a breathtaking canopy over the buzzing restaurants and breweries of the Howard Smith Wharves precinct directly beneath it.

Bet you never knew: The Story Bridge was designed by Dr John Bradfield, the exact same engineer who designed the iconic Sydney Harbour Bridge.

City Hall & Clocktower

Dominating King George Square in the heart of the CBD, Brisbane City Hall is the city's premier heritage landmark and civic heart. Opened in 1930, this striking Neoclassical building is affectionately known as "The People's Place". It is celebrated for its towering 87.47-metre Clock Tower and its completely free historical and cultural experiences. Below are some highlights:

  • The Vintage Cage Lift: Your journey begins by riding Queensland's oldest operating, hand-operated vintage cage elevator. As you ascend, you pass right through the internal ticking gears and structural framing of the tower.
  • Observation Platform: Once at the top (64 metres high), you step out onto an open-air platform nestled directly behind the massive clock faces, offering 360-degree views looking down into King George Square and across the modern CBD skyline.
  • The Chimes: The tower houses a massive 4.3-tonne central bell and four quarter-bells that chime the Westminster melody every 15 minutes.
  • Pro Tip: The bells chime automatically during the week, making Monday to Friday the best time to visit if you want to see the mechanisms moving.
